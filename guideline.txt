PART 0 — FOUNDATIONAL RULES BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE
0.1 What the Report Must Demonstrate
The guidelines state four explicit things the report must show. Every section you write must be measurable against all four:
1. You understand the wider context of computing by relating your project and approach to existing products or research. This is why the literature review exists, why your design must cite prior work, and why your evaluation must compare against existing techniques.
2. You can apply theoretical and practical degree-level techniques to produce a significant piece of written work and a substantial piece of software. Significant and substantial are the operative words — modest scope cannot achieve A++.
3. You are capable of objectively criticising your own work, discussing its implications, and making constructive suggestions for improvements. Objective self-criticism is explicitly required. Claiming success without identifying limitations is a direct failure against this criterion.
4. As a computing professional, you can explain your thinking clearly and concisely to third parties who may not be experts. Your report must be readable by an informed non-specialist. Assume your reader is a computer scientist, not a specialist in your exact subfield.
0.2 The Fundamental Priority Hierarchy
The guidelines state this explicitly and students consistently get it wrong:

"You should consider that the aim of the project is to produce a good report and that software, hardware, theory, etc. that you developed during the project are merely a means to this end."

The report is the product. The software/system/experiment is evidence within the report. A perfect system with a poor report scores poorly. A modest system with an excellent report scores well. Internalise this now.
0.3 Who Reads Your Report

Your supervisor and a second marker assess the full report
External examiners only have the report — no demonstration, no code, no conversation. For degree classification boundaries and prize nominations, the report is the sole artefact
Most project assessors will not have followed the project throughout — they rely on the report alone

Every section must be comprehensible to a marker who has never spoken to you and is reading your report cold.
0.4 The Word Limit — Hard Constraint

Main body: must not exceed 25,000 words
Exceeding this = zero mark on the entire project
Appendices are excluded from the word count
Expected length: considerably less than 25,000 words
Conciseness and clarity are explicitly rewarded; padding is explicitly penalised

PART 3 — THE KCL GENERIC MARKING CRITERIA (LEVEL 6 UG) — A++ STANDARD ONLY
The College Marking Framework applies four generic dimensions to all Level 6 assessments alongside the project-specific rubric.
Dimension 1: Subject Knowledge & Understanding — A++ Standard
Highly detailed systematic understanding of the key concepts, terminology and theories which form the knowledge base of the subject and/or specialist area. Fully informed by specialist and/or new reading. Critical appreciation of competing theories, principles and concepts. Insight in recognition of the interrelationship between fields of study.
Dimension 2: Intellectual Skills — A++ Standard
Takes an insightful critical approach with convincing, well-synthesised abstract arguments throughout. An independent well-defined focus of research enquiry. Discerning selection and application of strategies to address complex problems, showing originality. Extensive range of sources evaluated, referenced and applied to unfamiliar situations with flexibility, of excellent quality. Excellent discrimination between relevance of data/theories, analysis of new theory/data, showing critical judgement and originality/creativity.
Dimension 3: Practical Skills including Digital Capabilities — A++ Standard
Highly proficient and flexible application of discipline-specific specialist skills. Processing and interpretation of data efficiently to generate new information. Proficient use of relevant computational tools and technologies across all relevant contexts. Exceptional digital literacy skills with critical appreciation and application.
Dimension 4: Generic/Transferable Skills including Professional Competencies — A++ Standard
Excellent communication of information and ideas. Excellent awareness of audience and appropriate style maintained throughout. Excellent demonstration of insight and autonomy in evaluating own strengths, weaknesses, and impact. Applies critical and technical reflection to set goals and plans for enhancement. Highly developed ethical accountability in relation to professional and personal codes of conduct.
Practical implication: Your LSESP chapter, honest evaluation of limitations, and reflective conclusions all contribute to Dimension 4. They are independently scored, not decorative.


PART 4 — MANDATORY REPORT STRUCTURE
4.1 Required Elements in Order
1. Title page — project title, author name, supervisor name (optional), student ID, date, module code (6CCS3PRJ).
2. Originality Avowal — must contain verbatim: "I verify that I am the sole author of this report, except where explicitly stated to the contrary." Followed by signature and date. Confirm word count does not exceed 25,000 words.
3. Abstract — approximately half a page. A person unfamiliar with your project must have a good idea of what your work is about from this alone. States: problem, method, key findings including negative findings. Self-contained — no forward references.
4. Acknowledgements — brief thanks to supervisor and others who assisted.
5. Contents Page — all chapters and subsections with page numbers. Stick to sections and subsections — subsubsections are usually avoidable. Include List of Figures if 8+ figures.
6. Introduction — see Part 5.1.
7. Background / Literature Review — see Part 5.2.
8. Body Chapters (typically 3–4 chapters) — design, implementation, methodology, experiments, testing. Structure is project-dependent.
9. Evaluation — see Part 5.3.
10. Legal, Social, Ethical, Sustainability and Professional Issues — standalone chapter. See Part 5.4.
11. Conclusions and Future Work — see Part 5.5.
12. Bibliography — full bibliographic details for every cited source.
13. Appendices — source code, test cases, extended tables. Examiners are not obliged to read appendices. Do not put essential content here.
14. User Guide — required at supervisor's discretion. Walk-through of a typical session. Keep concise. Use diagrams. Can be a chapter or appendix.


PART 5 — SECTION-BY-SECTION RULES
5.1 Introduction Rules
Rule I-1: Must begin with a statement understandable by a lay reader — not a specialist, not someone in your exact subfield.
Rule I-2 (A++ criterion — the most explicitly stated differentiator in the entire rubric): The introduction must proceed from the wider concerns of Informatics to the specific subject via an orderly and natural progression of ideas. Broader field/societal context → subfield → specific problem → gap → this project's contribution → report structure. This phrase appears ONLY in the A++ descriptor.
Rule I-3: End with explicit pointers to every remaining chapter. "Chapter 2 reviews... Chapter 3 describes... Chapter 4 presents..."
Rule I-4: All four department sub-criteria must be satisfied:

Context for the work ✓
Problem clearly defined ✓
Importance motivated ✓
Relevance established ✓

5.2 Literature Review Rules
Rule L-1: Organised thematically by concept/problem area — not as a sequential list of papers. Each section must carry an argument, not just a summary.
Rule L-2: Key technical issues must be summarised. For each area covered, the reader must know what the key challenges, trade-offs, and open problems are.
Rule L-3: Every paper cited must have a visible connection to your project. If you cannot explain why a paper is in your review, remove it.
Rule L-4: Sources must be critically analysed — evaluating strengths, weaknesses, limitations, assumptions, and applicability to your context. Not describing what each paper found — evaluating it.
Rule L-5: Coverage must be comprehensive. Seminal papers in your area plus recent work (within 5 years) must both be present. No major relevant work missing.
Rule L-6 (A++ criterion): The review must give new insights into the project area not immediately identifiable from the literature itself. Your synthesis must produce something no individual paper says — a gap, a contradiction, an unaddressed combination of problems — that motivates your specific design choices.
Rule L-7: The bridge from the review to the design must be explicitly written. Do not assume the reader will infer it. Write the sentence: "Based on the limitations identified in Section 2.X, specifically [Y], the following design requirements were established..."
Hard anti-pattern: "Smith et al. found X. Jones et al. found Y. Brown et al. found Z." — this is description, not critical analysis. It fails sub-criterion 4 regardless of how many papers you cite.
5.3 Design and Implementation Rules
Rule D-1: The design must be explicitly motivated by the literature review. The connection must be written, not implied.
Rule D-2 (explicit sub-criterion): Alternative designs must be considered. For every major design decision, at least one alternative must be named, described, and rejected with specific technical reasons.
Rule D-3: A rationale for the design approach must be given — why this approach over alternatives, what properties it has that matter for this problem, how it addresses the stated requirements.
Rule D-4: Every major technology, framework, library, algorithm, and data structure choice requires explicit justification referencing specific requirements or design properties.
Rule D-5: Testing must cover positive cases (expected behaviour), negative cases (error/edge cases), and boundary conditions. Testing must be shown, not just described.
Rule D-6 (A++ criterion — first and highest-priority implementation sub-criterion): The implemented product must be sophisticated. Scope and complexity must genuinely exceed the routine.
Rule D-7: Methodology must be sound and clearly presented — appropriate methods used, reasoning explained, someone else could reproduce the methodology from your description.
Rule D-8: Software maintenance must be explicitly discussed: dependency risks, modularity/extensibility, technical debt acknowledged, how a future developer would modify the system.
Rule D-9 (A++ criterion): There must be something distinctly ambitious about what was attempted, carried off with style, with problems rarely defeating the student and clever solutions being the norm. Ambitious scope + full execution = A++. Either alone is insufficient.
Rule D-10: When implementation deviates from the design plan, document the deviation explicitly — original plan, change made, reason, whether the original goal was still achieved.
5.4 Evaluation Rules
The guidelines issue this explicit warning:

"Be warned that many projects fall down through poor evaluation. Simply building a system and documenting its design and functionality is not enough to gain a good mark."


"Avoid statements like 'The project has been a complete success and all of the problems in this area of computer science are now solved' — you will be shot down immediately!"


"There is no such thing as a perfect project. Even the very best pieces of work have their limitations and you are expected to provide a proper critical appraisal of what you have done."

Rule E-1: Results must be analysed and discussed — not reported. "Accuracy was 94%" is reporting. "The 94% accuracy indicates X because Y, consistent with/contradicting prior work Z" is analysis.
Rule E-2: Evaluation procedures must be explained before results are shown — what metrics, why those metrics, what baselines, why those baselines.
Rule E-3 (explicit sub-criterion): Material must be compared and contrasted — against prior work from the literature review, against a null/random baseline, against your stated requirements.
Rule E-4 (explicit sub-criterion): Comparison of achievements with requirements must be present — a requirements traceability assessment is the gold standard.
Rule E-5 (explicit sub-criterion): Awareness of potential extensions must be shown — what could be done next and why, derived from the evaluation findings.
Rule E-6 (explicit sub-criterion): Reflective and justified conclusions must be drawn — what did the results teach you about the problem, the approach, or the field? Supported by evidence.
Rule E-7 (A++ criterion — most commonly missed): The evaluation must compare results expected from theory including those for alternative designs with those obtained in practice. Return to the alternatives rejected in the design chapter. Demonstrate — empirically or analytically — why your chosen design is superior. This phrase appears ONLY in the A++ descriptor.
Rule E-8: Evaluation must be both absolute (how good is this in absolute terms?) and comparative (how does this compare to existing techniques, software, etc.?). Both are required.
5.5 LSESP Rules
Rule LS-1: Standalone chapter with a reasoned discussion. Not a subsection. Reasoned means each point is argued, not listed.
Rule LS-2: BCS Code of Conduct must be explicitly referenced and applied to this project. All four areas: (1) Public Interest, (2) Professional Competence & Integrity, (3) Duty to Relevant Authority, (4) Duty to Colleagues — each connected to your specific project.
Rule LS-3: All three department sub-criteria must be satisfied:

Relevant professional standards indicated ✓
Social and ethical implications outlined ✓
Thoughtful discussion of project impact: public well-being, environment, sustainability, economic, commercial factors ✓

Rule LS-4 (A++ criterion): The discussion must cover ALL relevant aspects: public well-being, computer security, environmental and sustainability aspects, economic and commercial factors, globalization, accessibility, IP and related issues, software trustworthiness. The word "all" appears only at A++.
Rule LS-5: Domain-specific ethical issues unique to your project must be identified. Generic BCS + GDPR that applies to any software project is insufficient for A++. Domain-specific issues by project type:

ML/AI: algorithmic bias, training data bias, dual-use potential, explainability
Security research: dual-use implications (weakness identification enables both defence and attack)
Healthcare/clinical data: informed consent, anonymisation, impact on patient outcomes
Educational tools: accuracy of presented information, accessibility, pedagogical ethics
Knowledge/data systems: data representativeness, language accessibility, data sovereignty

Rule LS-6: Legislation must be cited directly. Name the specific regulation, cite the specific articles that apply, explain your project's compliance with those articles.
5.6 Conclusions and Future Work Rules
The guidelines provide explicit examples and explicit bans.
Good conclusions (from guidelines):

"The use of overloading in C++ provides a very elegant mechanism for transparent parallelisation of sequential programs"
"The overheads of linear-time n-body algorithms makes them computationally less efficient than O(n log n) algorithms for systems with less than 100000 particles"

Banned (from guidelines verbatim):

"I learned a lot about C++ programming..."
"Simulating colliding galaxies can be real fun..."

These are transferable principles about the field vs personal reflections. Only the former belongs in conclusions.
Rule C-1: Conclusions must state what was learned, not what was built. "We built a system that does X" is a summary, not a conclusion.
Rule C-2: Future work must be specific enough to function as one of: a plan for making the software marketable, ideas for an MPhil/PhD continuation, or a discussion of how the project could accommodate future requirements. Not generic improvement wishes.
Rule C-3 (A++ criterion): Conclusions must make accurate predictions of results that would be obtained by applying the present work in new areas. This appears ONLY in the A++ descriptor. Forward-looking analytical predictions: "If this approach were applied to [different domain], we would predict [specific outcome] because [reasoning from our findings]."
Rule C-4: Limitations must include: what the limitation is, why it exists (traceable to a design decision or constraint), and what could be done to address it.
Rule C-5: No personal reflections — not in conclusions, not anywhere in the report.


PART 6 — THE SEVEN NON-NEGOTIABLE 90%+ TRAITS
These seven characteristics appear only in the A++ descriptor and are absent from all lower grade levels. Missing any single one makes 90%+ unachievable regardless of everything else.
Trait 1: The Introduction Proceeds from Wider Informatics to Specific Subject
Exact rubric wording: "Proceeds from the wider concerns of Informatics to the specific subject of the report by way of an orderly and natural progression of ideas."
This phrase appears ONLY in the A++ Introduction descriptor. Broader field/societal context → subfield → specific problem → gap → this project. A reader is carried from broad to specific without jumps.
Test: Read your introduction's first three paragraphs. If they begin with your specific system or project, rewrite. They must begin with the broader field.
Trait 2: The Literature Review Gives New Insights
Exact rubric wording: "The Review is a penetrating analysis, showing an impressive grasp of the subject and excellent judgement in the selection of previous work that should be applied in project. It gives new insights into the project area, not immediately identifiable from literature."
Your synthesis must produce something the individual papers don't say alone. Not achievable by reading more papers — achievable by thinking across papers to identify gaps, contradictions, or patterns they individually don't reveal.
Test: Read the last paragraph of your literature review. Does it state an insight you arrived at through synthesis — not one stated in any single paper? If no, rewrite.
Trait 3: The Project Is Genuinely Ambitious and Fully Executed
Exact rubric wording: "There is something distinctly ambitious about what is being attempted and the student has carried off the attempt with style, rarely defeated by problems encountered and more typically finding some clever solution."
Both words matter: ambitious (scope exceeds the routine) AND carried off (execution matches the scope). Ambitious scope poorly executed or modest scope excellently executed — neither is A++. Must be both.
Test: Would a knowledgeable CS marker describe this project as ambitious? Does the implementation show clever solutions to non-trivial problems?
Trait 4: The Evaluation Covers Alternative Designs
Exact rubric wording: "Evaluates the artefact, comparing results expected from theory (including those for alternative designs) with those obtained in practice."
The phrase "including those for alternative designs" appears ONLY in the A++ Evaluation descriptor. Return to the alternatives rejected in the design chapter. Demonstrate — with evidence or analytical argument — why your chosen design is superior.
Test: Does your evaluation chapter contain a section explicitly addressing the alternative designs from your design chapter and explaining why your approach outperforms them based on results?
Trait 5: Every Design Decision Is Traceable Across the Report
Source: KCL Generic Marking Criteria Level 6, Intellectual Skills dimension.
Every major design decision must trace along this chain without gaps: literature gap → requirement → design choice → implementation → test → evaluation result.
Test: Pick any major design decision. Can you trace it from a specific literature review finding, through a numbered requirement, through a design justification, to a specific test, to a specific evaluation result? If any link is missing, fix it.
Trait 6: Conclusions State Intellectual Learnings with Forward Predictions
Exact rubric wording: "States clear conclusions from the work done, making accurate predictions of results that would be obtained by applying the present work in new areas."
Intellectual learnings are transferable principles about the field (see the guidelines' C++ overloading example). Forward predictions specify what would happen if the work were applied to a different domain, with reasoning. Both are required. Neither a project summary nor personal reflections qualify.
Test: Read each conclusion. Could it appear in a research paper's conclusion section? Does it state a principle about the field? Does it make a specific prediction for a new area? If it reads as a student project reflection, rewrite it.
Trait 7: LSESP Covers All Relevant Aspects with Domain-Specific Depth
Exact rubric wording: "Thoughtful discussion of all relevant aspects such as: effects on the public well-being, computer security, environmental and sustainability aspects, economic and commercial factors, globalization, accessibility, Intellectual Property and related issues, software trustworthiness."
"All" is the operative word. Generic BCS + GDPR treatment does not achieve A++. Domain-specific, project-specific ethical analysis of all dimensions does.
Test: Could your LSESP chapter be transplanted into a different dissertation without changing anything? If yes, it's not specific enough for A++.


PART 7 — WRITING RULES
W-1: Every claim needs evidence. Citation, empirical evidence, or explicit logical argument. No unsupported assertions.
W-2: Justification language.

BANNED: "X was chosen because it is widely used / popular / easy to use / natural choice"
REQUIRED: "X was chosen because [specific technical property] satisfies [specific requirement Rn identified in Section Y]. [Alternative Z] was considered and rejected because [specific technical limitation W]."

W-3: Results must be interpreted not reported.

BANNED: "Table 3 shows accuracy of 94.2%."
REQUIRED: "The 94.2% accuracy demonstrates [interpretation], which is consistent with/contradicts [specific prior work from Section 2.X] because [reasoning]."

W-4: Limitations must have structure.

BANNED: "The system has some limitations."
REQUIRED: "[Limitation X] occurs because [cause Y], stemming from [design decision in Section Z]. Addressable in future work by [specific approach W], requiring [condition V]."

W-5: Negative results are required. The guidelines explicitly state you will be shot down for claiming complete success. Intellectual honesty is directly assessed under criterion 3 of Part 0.1.
W-6: Future work must be specific and derived.

BANNED: "Future work could improve scalability / performance / accuracy."
REQUIRED: "Future work should evaluate [specific technique W] applied to [specific problem] to test [specific hypothesis H], addressing the limitation identified in Section 5.2. Validated using [specific methodology]."

W-7: Abstract is self-contained. Problem, method, key findings including negative findings. No forward references. No vague statements.
W-8: Tone. Academic, precise, third-person in analytical sections. First-person acceptable in motivation and reflection only. Never lay language where precise technical vocabulary exists.
W-9: Figures and tables. Every figure must be: (1) referenced in text before it appears, (2) captioned to convey meaning independently, (3) accompanied by explanatory prose telling the reader what to observe.
W-10: Literature review is thematic not chronological. Organised by concept or problem area. Every section concludes with a statement connecting to the gap your project addresses.
W-11: The bridge must be stated. Write the sentence explicitly: "Based on the limitations identified in Section 2.X, specifically [Y], the following design requirements were established..."


PART 8 — ANTI-PATTERNS
8.1 Anti-Patterns That Prevent 90%+
1. Introduction starting at the project level. Beginning with "In this project I built..." rather than the wider field context. The single most explicitly stated A++ criterion and the most commonly missed.
2. Literature review that doesn't bridge to the design. Even an excellent review loses its value if the design chapter begins without referencing the gaps or findings identified. The bridge sentence must be written.
3. Evaluation that doesn't cover alternative designs. The explicit A++ evaluation criterion. Work in the 75–85% range typically evaluates only the implemented system. A++ work returns to the design chapter and evaluates alternatives.
4. Descriptive literature review. Papers summarised one by one without synthesis. Fails sub-criterion 4 (critically analysed) regardless of how many papers are cited.
5. Design choices without alternatives. The explicit sub-criterion is "alternative designs have been considered." A design chapter that only describes the chosen approach fails this criterion.
6. Generic future work. "Improve scalability" is a wish, not future work. Must be specific, named, derived from the project's actual findings, and actionable.
7. Conclusions that describe rather than learn. Describing what was built in the conclusions is repetition of the implementation chapter, not intellectual contribution.
8. LSESP interchangeable with any other project. If it doesn't identify domain-specific issues and cover all dimensions, it does not achieve A++.
9. Claiming complete success. The guidelines explicitly warn against this. Markers will identify your limitations — you must identify them first and more rigorously.
10. Personal reflections in conclusions. Explicitly banned in the guidelines. "I learned a lot about X" and "this project was rewarding" are not conclusions.

PART 9 — SURFACE-LEVEL VS DEEP WORK
Drawn from patterns across the five sample dissertations. Surface = typical 65–75%. Deep = 85–90%+.

Design Justification
SURFACE: "ResNet was chosen because it performs well on image tasks and is widely used in the literature."
DEEP: "ResNet was chosen over VGG-16 and InceptionV3 because its residual connections mitigate vanishing gradients in networks deeper than 50 layers [ref], critical given the depth requirement identified in Section 2.3. VGG-16 was eliminated due to its 3–4× higher parameter count at equivalent accuracy, exceeding the inference latency requirement NFR3. InceptionV3 was considered but its branched architecture is incompatible with the Grad-CAM visualisation method required by FR7."

Literature Review Synthesis
SURFACE: "Several papers have proposed methods for fairness in facial detection. Smith (2020) proposed pre-processing augmentation. Jones (2021) proposed threshold adjustment. Brown (2022) found that environmental variables affect performance."
DEEP: "Existing fairness interventions fall into two categories: pre-processing approaches (data augmentation and resampling) and post-processing approaches (threshold adjustment). Pre-processing achieves demographic parity at the cost of 3–8% overall accuracy degradation (Smith, 2020; Jones, 2021). Post-processing preserves accuracy but requires demographic labels at inference time, violating privacy constraints in most deployment contexts (Brown, 2022). Neither category addresses the root cause: environmental variable sensitivity (brightness, contrast) affects dark-skinned faces disproportionately due to training data distribution bias. This gap — fairness intervention without demographic labelling or accuracy loss — motivates the photo-editing approach in Chapter 3."

Testing
SURFACE: "We wrote 50 unit tests that all pass, confirming the system works correctly."
DEEP: "Unit tests were designed to exercise boundary conditions at the input preprocessing stage, edge cases in the author-lookup function (empty commit histories, commits with redacted emails), and error propagation across the REST routes. For each route, both positive cases (using real, pre-written phenotypes) and negative cases (using false repository names, malformed request bodies, and expired authentication tokens) were tested. The 821 tests achieve full route coverage. Quality assurance tables (Tables 5.1–5.3) rate each requirement's implementation strength; UR4 and AR4 are rated Moderate because step-file retrieval requires two sequential routes rather than a single operation — a limitation discussed in Section 5.2."

Evaluation
SURFACE: "Our system achieves 94% accuracy, which demonstrates strong performance."
DEEP: "The 94.2% accuracy of the unmitigated classifier under the paper's original settings (Figure 5.1, purple line) is consistent with the 93.4% reported in the original MaMaDroid paper [13], validating our experimental implementation. Removing the spatial and temporal bias identified in Section 2.1.2 reduces performance to 28.4% AUT, a 69.1% decrease. This degradation aligns precisely with the bias removal findings in the TESSERACT paper [14], providing independent validation of their methodology. The base performance of 28.4% is meaningfully above the random baseline of 16.4%, indicating the classifier retains genuine discriminative capability even under realistic conditions, but well below the operational requirement of >80% AUT established in Section 3.1."

Conclusions
SURFACE: "In conclusion, this project successfully built a web-based system for teaching binary relations in Discrete Mathematics. The three interactive tools were implemented and all functional requirements were met."
DEEP: "The key findings establish that: (1) web-based interactive learning tools can achieve sub-second response times for relation sets up to 50 elements with client-side computation, suggesting server-side computation is unnecessary for this complexity class; (2) the accessibility-performance trade-off is near-zero for interactive graph rendering at WCAG AA compliance, contradicting the common assumption that accessibility features significantly impact performance; (3) step-by-step algorithm visualisation produces measurably better student comprehension than end-state display, as evidenced by the competency question results in Section 8.5. Applied to broader educational CS tooling, these findings predict that client-side interactive visualisation can replace server-side computation for graph algorithms with sets up to approximately 100 nodes, enabling simpler and more accessible deployments without performance trade-off."

LSESP
SURFACE: "This project follows GDPR and the BCS Code of Conduct. Data is processed securely and the system is designed with public benefit in mind."
DEEP: "This project processes [specific data type] which constitutes personal data under GDPR Article 4(1) [EU Regulation 2016/679].
